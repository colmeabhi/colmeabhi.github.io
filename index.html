Work

  1. SDE-Platform II, Quantiphi

    Jan 2024 to Jun 2025

    I was on the platform team for a large insurer's data platform. I designed the move of its ETL off Informatica, made day-long loads restartable, and built a self-service tool that gave about fifty engineers infrastructure on request.

    Read moreShow less

    The ETL move started with a design and a cost model. I wrote both and took them through review with the platform, data, business and finance people. Lambda and Fargate, run by Step Functions, replaced the Informatica servers, and on paper that cuts compute cost by about 70%. That is a projection, not a bill.

    The part I liked most was smaller. Full loads took a whole day, so a failure late in the run meant starting over and hitting the source databases twice the next day. I split them into batches of about two hours that restart on their own, so a failure only reruns its own batch. We met the next-day deadline on 95%+ of runs while the data doubled to about 600 GB a day. Most of the misses came from other teams changing their source databases, which is a sentence I said in a lot of meetings.

    The self-service tool let about fifty data engineers get their own test infrastructure by asking for it, with an LLM in the middle that was only allowed to fill in blanks. There is a longer write-up on it.

    I was also on call for six services. Putting all three environments on one Grafana dashboard, with alerts on what users actually felt, took a typical fix from about three hours to about ten minutes. Releases went out every two weeks, and a release that failed its checks rolled itself back to the last version that worked. I mentored two junior engineers for a year and handed the platform over to a five-person support team.

Projects

driftfix

hackathon winner, then a team of three

Someone fixes a production incident at 2 am by clicking around the AWS console, and nobody updates the template afterwards. On the next deploy CloudFormation quietly undoes the fix, and everyone gets to enjoy the same incident twice.

Read moreShow less

driftfix reads CloudTrail, works out what was changed by hand, asks an LLM on Bedrock to update the template, checks the result with cfn-lint and opens a pull request. It never touches infrastructure and never merges anything. A person still decides.

It started as Phantom, which won the AWS x Datadog GenAI hackathon. Three of us then rebuilt it properly as a multi-tenant service. I led that, and my part was the architecture, the Lambda pipeline, the cross-account IAM and the Terraform. In load tests ten stacks ran in parallel with zero errors. The slow part turned out to be the LLM, not us.

Cache-aware scale-in

M.S. project, in progress

When an LLM deployment scales down, Kubernetes has to pick a replica to delete, and it more or less picks the newest one. It has no idea that pod might be the one holding the cache everybody is using.

Read moreShow less

I wrote a small controller that scores each replica's cache and tells Kubernetes which one is cheapest to lose.

I have run the benchmark on three A100s. The controller removed the pod it was meant to, and it made no difference, because my test traffic warmed every replica's cache to about 95%. The fix is a harsher test and a better score, and that is what I am working on now.

Parallel N-body simulation

A galaxy simulation that I wrote five different ways: one thread, OpenMP, Barnes-Hut, and two CUDA kernels. Same physics every time, so the only thing that changes is how the work is split up.

Read moreShow less

Along the way I found a data race in my own GPU kernel. Some bodies were feeling forces from the past and some from the future, which is not how gravity works. Splitting the update across two kernel launches fixed it.
